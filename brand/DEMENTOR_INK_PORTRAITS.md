# Dementor Ink — Portrait Canon

Status: **approved / canonical**  
Scope: portraits and portrait-led illustrations for Dementor Club  
Branch: `dementor-club`

## Core direction

Portraits must move away from polished watercolor realism and toward **raw hand-drawn ink graphics with controlled accidents**.

The portrait is not a photo filter. It is a reinterpretation of the person through line, silhouette, distortion, reduction and ink behavior.

## Face

- Keep the face **simplified and graphic**.
- Use a mostly **flat skin tone** rather than painterly modelling.
- Draw the face as if it were made quickly by hand with ink: one dominant contour and a minimum number of internal lines.
- Preserve recognizability through 3–5 anchors only: silhouette, hairline, nose, eyes, mouth, characteristic proportions.
- Avoid photorealistic skin texture, soft airbrushed shadows, smooth gradients and over-rendering.
- Allow asymmetry, omissions, imperfect contour closure and visible searching lines.

## Facial shadow

Facial shadows are shown primarily through **hand-scratched linear ink hatching**:

- sparse short strokes;
- uneven spacing;
- variable pressure;
- slightly nervous direction;
- occasional crossing and overrun;
- no smooth tonal gradient;
- no clean academic cross-hatching across the whole face.

The shadow should look as if the artist quickly scratched it in with a pen rather than carefully modelled volume.

## Line

The line must be:

- hand-made;
- irregular;
- variable in weight;
- occasionally broken;
- occasionally doubled;
- allowed to run past the form;
- sometimes thin and precise, sometimes thick and almost becoming a blot.

Avoid uniform digital outlines and clean vector-like closure.

## Ink masses

Use **bold black ink fills** as an essential compositional element, not decoration.

Allowed and encouraged:

- large irregular black masses;
- overfilled zones;
- dry-brush edges;
- ink bleeding;
- accidental-looking overlaps;
- black areas that partially swallow a contour;
- asymmetrical placement.

The work should contain at least one area where line becomes mass.

## Accidents

Controlled accidents are canonical:

- splashes of different sizes;
- long stray lines;
- drips;
- runs;
- smears;
- brush scratches;
- ink drops;
- lines detached from anatomy;
- marks that appear to come from the physical act of drawing.

Do not distribute them evenly. Build contrast between **quiet paper** and **violent ink activity**.

## Color

Color is secondary and should behave as an intervention rather than normal coloring.

Base:

- paper / warm white;
- black ink.

Accent colors may include:

- red;
- orange;
- muted blue;
- occasional dirty warm neutrals.

Rules:

- limited palette;
- color may leave the contour;
- color may be flat;
- color may be partially covered by black ink;
- avoid fully coloring every object according to reality.

## Composition

Prefer:

- large areas of untouched paper;
- asymmetry;
- cropped figures;
- off-center portraits;
- concentrated black mass in one zone;
- large negative space in another;
- visual tension rather than balanced poster symmetry.

## Degree of stylization

For portrait work, recognizability is required, but realism is not.

Target balance:

- recognizability: high enough to identify the person;
- anatomical fidelity: deliberately reduced;
- graphic deformation: visible;
- materiality of ink: strong;
- painterly realism: low.

## Anti-patterns

Do **not** use as the dominant treatment:

- polished watercolor portraiture;
- smooth skin shading;
- hyper-detailed facial rendering;
- clean comic-book contour;
- vector-like flat line of constant thickness;
- uniform splatter overlay;
- decorative grunge added after a clean illustration;
- evenly filled background texture.

## Canonical formula

**Recognizable person + simplified flat face + hand-drawn ink contour + scratched linear facial shadows + bold black fills + stray lines + drips + splashes + limited color accents + large untouched paper areas.**

## Photo-to-portrait workflow

1. Identify the 3–5 features that make the person recognizable.
2. Remove most photographic detail.
3. Choose one dominant deformation or emphasis.
4. Rebuild the silhouette first.
5. Draw the face with minimal ink lines.
6. Keep the face mostly flat in tone.
7. Add shadow only with irregular hand-scratched hatching.
8. Introduce one or more bold black ink masses.
9. Add stray lines, splashes and drips asymmetrically.
10. Add limited color only where it strengthens the image.
11. Stop before the image becomes polished.

## Quality test

A finished portrait is correct when:

- the person is still recognizable;
- the face does not look photographically modelled;
- the ink line visibly feels hand-made;
- black masses are bold enough to carry composition;
- accidental marks feel physical rather than like a software overlay;
- color is sparse;
- there is substantial untouched paper;
- the whole image feels more like an aggressive editorial drawing than a watercolor portrait.
