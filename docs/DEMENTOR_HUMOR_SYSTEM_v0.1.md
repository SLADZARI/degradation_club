## 0. Purpose

This document defines how humor should work across Dementor Club surfaces without turning the club into a generic joke feed.

Humor is not decoration. In Dementor Club it is a way to expose the contradiction between the rules people are told to follow and what those rules look like in ordinary life.

The core task is not `write a joke`. The core task is:

`REALITY -> SOCIAL SCRIPT -> ANOMALY -> COMIC ANGLE -> MECHANISM -> VOICE -> FORMAT -> COMPRESSION -> TEST`

The candidate must be tested before promotion to approved project authority.

---

## 1. Core brand function of humor

Dementor Club uses humor to:

1. make an invisible social rule visible;
2. show the absurdity of over-optimization, achievement culture and compulsory self-improvement;
3. create recognition — `this is exactly what happens`;
4. give the audience a compact phrase, image or object through which the phenomenon can be remembered;
5. lower lecture-tone while preserving the thought;
6. build a shared language and lore around the club.

Humor must not replace meaning. If removing the comic surface leaves no observation, the material is weak.

### Canonical working formula

`HUMOR VALUE = OBSERVATION × INCONGRUITY × REFRAMING × LANGUAGE × SHARED CONTEXT`

Persona, visual grammar and timing are multipliers.

If one core factor is near zero, additional punchlines usually do not rescue the piece.

---

## 2. Dementor default comic position

The club is not a comedian standing outside the audience and mocking it.

Default position:

**we are inside the same absurd system and have noticed how strange it is.**

Preferred targets:
- systems;
- rituals;
- social expectations;
- productivity language;
- management language;
- self-help language;
- consumer promises;
- technology rituals;
- our own behavior;
- the club itself.

Avoid making ordinary vulnerability the primary target. Punching down is not a Dementor mechanism; exposing a system or shared contradiction is.

## 3. Russian-language voice rules

### 3.1 Recognition before cleverness

The first question is not `is this witty?` but `is the observation true and recognizable?`.

Preferred source material:
- something people repeatedly do but rarely name;
- a phrase people use automatically;
- a rule that contradicts behavior;
- a status claim contradicted by reality;
- a process that exists to manage another process;
- a tool that creates the problem it claims to solve;
- a serious institution applied to a trivial human situation;
- a trivial household logic applied to something grand.

### 3.2 Register collision

Russian Dementor humor may deliberately switch registers:
- official/bureaucratic -> domestic;
- corporate -> emotional;
- scientific -> primitive;
- therapeutic -> administrative;
- heroic -> mundane;
- motivational -> exhausted;
- sacred/manifesto-like -> petty everyday reality;
- colloquial -> institutional.

The collision must reveal the contradiction, not exist only as verbal decoration.

### 3.3 Deadpan first

Default delivery is calm. The text should rarely announce that it is joking.

Prefer:
- short declarative phrases;
- pseudo-serious wording;
- understatement;
- factual tone applied to absurd logic;
- a final line that closes the thought without explaining it.

Avoid:
- `шутка в том, что...`;
- explaining the punch after the punch;
- emoji as a substitute for comic meaning;
- excessive exclamation marks;
- generic stand-up filler;
- forced slang merely to sound young.

### 3.4 Anti-AI syntax filter

Treat the following as warning patterns, not absolute bans:
- `Когда ты настолько X, что Y`;
- `Это не X, это Y` used mechanically;
- `Я: ... / Также я: ...` without a fresh observation;
- generic `plot twist` wording;
- motivational cliché with one antonym swapped;
- joke that can be moved unchanged to any topic.

If the syntax is predictable, the observation or language must be unusually strong.

### 3.5 Compression

Delete any phrase the audience can infer.

For meme, merch and headline surfaces, the ideal text usually contains one observation and one reframe, not a mini-essay.

## 4. Primary Dementor humor mechanisms

These are mechanisms, not genres. A piece may combine 1–3, but should have one dominant mechanism.

### M01 · SOCIAL CONTRADICTION

Find a gap between declared rule and actual behavior.

Input: `what people say / what actually happens`.
Transform: expose both in one frame.
Best for: Home, About, social, projects, courses.
Risk: becoming moralistic.
Test: does the audience recognize the contradiction before being told what to think?

### M02 · REGISTER SHIFT — HIGH -> LOW

Describe something elevated through mundane language.

Best for: self-development, success, meaning, relationships, events.
Risk: mere vulgarization without insight.

### M03 · REGISTER SHIFT — LOW -> HIGH

Describe an ordinary weakness as policy, doctrine, protocol, scientific finding, strategy or institutional process.

Best for: social posts, objects, merch, onboarding, community.
Risk: repetitive fake-bureaucracy voice.

### M04 · LITERALIZATION

Take a metaphor, business phrase or self-help instruction literally.

Best for: short text, dialogue, carousel, course interactions.
Risk: cheap pun if there is no second layer.

### M05 · NAMING

Give a compact name to a recognizable phenomenon that lacks one.

Pattern: `phenomenon -> new term -> minimal definition/example`.
Best for: social, projects, community lore, merch.
Strong outcome: the term can be reused without its original explanation.
Risk: invented word nobody wants to repeat.

### M06 · INVERSION

Reverse a normal value hierarchy or causality while keeping internal logic.

This is a core Dementor mechanism because the brand itself is an inversion of the self-development industry.

Best for: Home, About, project concepts, campaigns, merch.
Risk: automatic antonyms without observation.

### M07 · LOGICAL OVERCOMMITMENT

Take a reasonable rule and apply it too consistently until its absurd implication appears.

Best for: Logic & Awareness, technology, control, productivity, courses.
Risk: long setup.

### M08 · HYPERBOLE / ESCALATION

Push a real behavior beyond the reasonable boundary.

Best for: reels, sketches, carousels, events.
Risk: loudness replacing accuracy.

### M09 · UNDERSTATEMENT

Describe an obviously significant failure, contradiction or chaos as a minor operational detail.

Best for: deadpan posts, technology, work, events, community.
Risk: context may be insufficient for decoding.

### M10 · RHETORICAL QUESTION

Let an apparently reasonable question expose the absurd premise.

Best for: educational projects, course screens, captions.
Risk: becoming preachy or predictable.

### M11 · CULTURAL ALLUSION

Use a shared phrase, genre, public ritual, media form or generational code to activate another scenario.

Best for: social and campaign work.
Rule: the allusion must add meaning, not merely prove cultural awareness.
Risk: high decode cost / fast aging.

### M12 · PSEUDO-DOCUMENT

Package the absurdity as a memo, instruction, protocol, warning, vacancy, report, policy, certificate, technical specification or public notice.

Best for: Logic & Awareness, social, objects, events, courses.
Risk: the form becomes the joke every time.

### M13 · PSEUDO-NEWS / OFFICIAL REPORT

Treat a mundane personal event as news or an institutional event as an absurdly neutral fact.

Best for: topical social posts.
Risk: confusing fiction with real reporting; factual context must remain clear where it matters.

### M14 · DIALOGIC REFRAME

A second voice interprets the first voice in an unexpected but internally correct way.

Best for: comments, short video, community, character content.
Risk: sitcom-generic dialogue.

### M15 · CALLBACK / RUNNING GAG

Return a known term, character, object or rule in a new context.

Best for: community and club lore.
Rule: callback compounds meaning; it should not be repeated solely for familiarity.

### M16 · WORLD / LORE LOGIC

Build a stable absurd world with rules, institutions and objects that the audience gradually learns.

Best for: Dementor personas, fake institutions, objects, campaigns, recurring social series.
Risk: lore becomes inaccessible to newcomers.
Requirement: every piece should still contain an entry point for someone who does not know the full universe.

### M17 · MEME GRAMMAR

Use the semantic structure of a meme rather than copying a famous caption.

Examples of grammar classes:
- reject / accept;
- declared priority / actual temptation;
- disaster / calm narration;
- expectation / observed result;
- before / after;
- authority / subordinate interpretation;
- two competing internal voices.

Best for: social.
Risk: stale templates, imported English cadence, dependence on copyrighted/third-party visual identity.

### M18 · SELF-IMPLICATION

The club, author or persona is also caught by the same criticism.

Best for: About, community, social, dementor profiles.
Effect: reduces superiority and strengthens trust.

## 5. Surface matrix — where which humor belongs

Humor density is a product decision. Not every surface should maximize laughs.

### 5.1 Home `/`

Purpose: explain the club and move the user forward in 20–30 seconds.

Humor density: **MEDIUM**.
Primary mechanisms: M01, M05, M06, M03, M09.
Delivery: one strong line per block; headlines and concise supporting copy.

Must do:
- make the inversion instantly legible;
- preserve navigation clarity;
- create recognition before lore.

Do not:
- stack several jokes before explaining what the club is;
- make CTA labels hard to understand;
- require niche meme knowledge.

Existing architecture already uses this mode: `лучшая версия себя`, `отрицательный рост`, `логика — враг стабильности`, etc. The humor system should sharpen rather than replace that structure.

### 5.2 About `/about`

Purpose: explain philosophy and boundaries.

Humor density: **LOW–MEDIUM**.
Primary mechanisms: M01, M06, M18, M02/M03, occasional M09.
Delivery: a serious argument interrupted by precise comic formulations.

Rule: the reader must understand the philosophy even if every joke is removed.

Humor here proves the club can doubt itself. It must not turn the manifesto into parody-only copy.

### 5.3 Social posts / memes

Purpose: discovery, recognition, shareability, cultural participation.

Humor density: **HIGH**.
Primary mechanisms: all, especially M01, M05, M03, M07, M11, M12, M13, M14, M17, M18.

Preferred generation order:
`fresh observation -> angle -> format -> wording -> visual grammar`.

Not:
`popular meme template -> find something to write into it`.

Social has two layers:
- EVERGREEN: stable club observations and vocabulary;
- TREND: current meme/news/cultural reference.

Trend content must never silently rewrite evergreen canon.

### 5.4 Community / board

Purpose: participation and group language.

Humor density: **MEDIUM–HIGH, conversational**.
Primary mechanisms: M05, M14, M15, M16, M18, understatement.

Best form:
- short reactions;
- recurring terms;
- member-generated observations;
- callbacks;
- situational humor.

Rule: community humor should create `we recognize this`, not a contest for the harshest punchline.

### 5.5 Dementor profiles

Purpose: distinguish people/personas without inventing facts.

Humor density: **PERSONA-SPECIFIC**.
Primary mechanisms depend on the approved profile voice.

Rule: there is no universal `Dementor comedian voice` that overwrites an individual's real material.

The club may provide structural mechanics, but facts, biography and personal claims remain governed by the responsible profile source.

### 5.6 Projects

Purpose: independent cultural/satirical works within the ecosystem.

Humor density: **DEFINED BY PROJECT**.

For `Logic & Awareness` default preference:
- M01 social contradiction;
- M07 logical overcommitment;
- M10 rhetorical question;
- M12 pseudo-document;
- M13 pseudo-official framing;
- M06 inversion;
- M03 institutional register.

The joke should expose bad reasoning or the social comfort of avoiding reasoning. It should not simply tell the audience that other people are stupid.

Project editorial truth stays in its responsible branch; the Humor System provides methods, not facts.

### 5.7 Courses

Purpose: move a learner through an experience, not perform stand-up.

Humor density: **MEDIUM**, with peaks at transitions/rewards.
Primary mechanisms: M07, M10, M14, M03, M09, M15.

Use humor to:
- lower resistance;
- expose a cognitive contradiction;
- reward progress;
- give memorable labels;
- make feedback human.

Never let a joke obscure:
- what the user must do next;
- whether an answer is right/wrong where correctness matters;
- a safety-critical instruction;
- state/progress.

`Думай с опасностью` is the current registered course; its humor must follow its approved content and cannot be inferred from the title alone.

### 5.8 Merch

Purpose: turn an idea into an object.

Humor density: **HIGH, extremely compressed**.
Primary mechanisms: M05, M06, M03, M09, M18.

The line must work:
1. without explanation;
2. at physical viewing distance;
3. as identity signal;
4. without needing a caption.

Existing registered statements such as `OVERTHINKING IS MY CARDIO`, `PERSONAL GROWTH CANCELLED`, `SUCCESS IS BORING` demonstrate the intended compressed identity-function.

Rule: merch is not a repository for leftover social jokes. The phrase should survive repeated exposure.

### 5.9 Objects / artifacts

Purpose: physicalize club logic.

Humor density: **HIGH concept, LOW copy**.
Primary mechanisms: M03, M06, M12, M16.

Best case: the existence/function/specification of the object is the joke; text only completes it.

### 5.10 Events / offline

Purpose: make participation desirable and lower social friction.

Humor density: **MEDIUM**.
Primary mechanisms: M03, M06, M09, M12, M15.

Useful forms:
- ritual/institution parody;
- absurd programme names;
- deadpan logistics;
- recurring club lore.

Hard boundary: date, venue, price, registration state and safety/logistical instructions are factual surfaces and must remain clear.

### 5.11 Join / onboarding

Purpose: self-selection and successful entry.

Humor density: **LOW–MEDIUM**.
Primary mechanisms: M01, M18, M05, mild M03.

Rule: humor may characterize a question, but may not make the required action or answer meaning ambiguous.

The user should never fail onboarding because the interface preferred a punchline to clarity.

### 5.12 Transactional, support, legal, payment

Humor density: **ZERO–LOW**.

Clarity, trust, factual status and consequence outrank voice.

Allowed: small non-blocking microcopy where no factual interpretation is affected.
Not allowed: humor about payment success/failure, legal consent, privacy, account state, destructive actions or support instructions when it could change interpretation.

## 6. DC9 sphere mapping

The nine club spheres are content lenses, not nine separate comedy genres. Each sphere has preferred contradictions and mechanisms.

### Личность
Core contradiction: identity as endless project.
Preferred: self-implication, naming, inversion, therapeutic/corporate register collision.

### Работа
Core contradiction: activity/status/process vs actual result.
Preferred: bureaucracy, pseudo-document, understatement, social contradiction, logical overcommitment.

### Потребление
Core contradiction: purchase presented as transformation.
Preferred: fake product language, inversion, specification parody, naming.

### Отношения
Core contradiction: spontaneous human relation managed as optimization/project.
Preferred: dialogue, literalization, register collision, self-implication.

### Контроль
Core contradiction: systems created to reduce uncertainty creating more systems and anxiety.
Preferred: escalation, pseudo-protocol, logical overcommitment, understatement.

### Информация
Core contradiction: more information does not guarantee more understanding.
Preferred: logic traps, headlines, pseudo-news, naming, allusion, rhetorical question.

### Саморазвитие
Core contradiction: improvement becomes compulsory maintenance.
Preferred: core brand inversion, motivational-register parody, literalization, self-implication.

### Смысл
Core contradiction: existential questions packaged as productivity tasks/products.
Preferred: high/low register collision, understatement, absurd institutional framing.

### Технологии
Core contradiction: automation designed to simplify life generates new rituals/dependencies.
Preferred: literalization, logical overcommitment, pseudo-specification, deadpan failure report, naming.

Rule: sphere selection narrows the observation field. It does not automatically determine the punchline.

## 7. Format selection

Choose format after the comic angle is found.

### One-liner
Use when one reframe is enough.

### Two-line turn
Use when audience needs one expected interpretation before switch.

### Dialogue
Use when misunderstanding/reinterpretation between voices is the mechanism.

### Fake document
Use when institutional form itself amplifies the contradiction.

### Carousel
Use when one observation can escalate through 3–7 consequences.

### Meme
Use when the visual grammar compresses context better than prose.

### Short video / sketch
Use when character, timing, physical action or escalation is essential.

### Lore artifact
Use when repeated world rules create value across many publications.

### Long text
Use when the observation needs argument. Humor becomes punctuation inside the idea, not the entire structure.

## 8. Generation protocol

For each piece, fill the following internal fields before writing variants:

1. `SURFACE` — where it will live.
2. `SPHERE` — one primary DC9 sphere, optional secondary.
3. `AUDIENCE` — who must decode it.
4. `REALITY` — observable situation without jokes.
5. `SOCIAL_SCRIPT` — what is supposed to happen / what people claim.
6. `ANOMALY` — precise contradiction.
7. `TARGET` — system/self/ritual/object/language/persona.
8. `ANGLE` — new interpretation.
9. `MECHANISM` — one dominant M-code.
10. `VOICE` — club / project / persona / fake institution.
11. `CULTURAL_PACK` — what knowledge is required.
12. `FORMAT`.
13. `COMPRESSION_LIMIT` — target length.
14. `RISK` — ambiguity, cruelty, staleness, factual confusion.

Only then generate variants.

Recommended generation: 5 candidates using at least 3 different mechanisms. Do not produce five paraphrases of the same punchline.

## 9. Quality gate

Score each candidate 0–2.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation truth | generic/invented | plausible | sharply recognizable |
| Contradiction | absent | visible | clean and surprising |
| Reframe novelty | cliché | usable | fresh |
| Russian language | translated/AI cadence | natural | precise/quotable |
| Compression | bloated | acceptable | nothing removable |
| Dementor fit | generic joke | adjacent | unmistakably club logic |
| Surface fit | blocks task | neutral | strengthens task |
| Decode cost | unclear | needs context | instantly decodable by target |
| Reuse/quote potential | none | moderate | phrase/format can travel |
| Risk control | harmful/confusing | manageable | target/risk clean |

Maximum: 20.

Candidate recommendation:
- 17–20: strong test candidate;
- 14–16: revise/test;
- 10–13: observation may be useful, joke is not ready;
- <10: discard or restart from REALITY.

A high score does not make a rule canonical; audience testing remains separate evidence.

## 10. Failure modes

Reject or rewrite when:

1. There is a punchline but no observation.
2. The text explains its own joke.
3. The joke works only because the target is weak/vulnerable.
4. The club sounds superior to the audience rather than implicated with it.
5. A generic English meme cadence was translated into Russian.
6. Bureaucratic parody is used again only because it is easy.
7. The meme template is doing all the work.
8. The cultural reference requires more explanation than the joke contains.
9. The joke damages a factual CTA, price, date, status, instruction or legal meaning.
10. Every variant has the same syntax.
11. The piece is `Dementor` only because the word `деградация` was inserted.
12. Negating a self-help slogan is mistaken for a complete idea.
13. The format is selected before a contradiction is found.

## 11. Evidence / research boundary

External humor corpora and repositories are REFERENCE inputs only. They can teach mechanisms, annotation schemes, dialogue patterns, sarcasm perception, meme grammar and publishing workflow.

They must not become the Dementor voice by copying examples or averaging datasets.

Priority reference classes for continued research:
- Russian wordplay annotation: KoWit-24;
- Russian conversational/joke corpora: Koziev datasets;
- Russian humor-generation experiments: Non-human-humor;
- sarcasm/irony mechanism annotation: iSarcasm, SPIRS, Sarcasm Corpus V2;
- meme grammar/caption structure: DeepHumor;
- agentic meme workflow: Hermes meme-generation skill;
- publication workflow: social-poster.

Generic English joke corpora are benchmarking material, not primary voice training material.
