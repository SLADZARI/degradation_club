# DEMENTOR CLUB — BUSINESS VALIDATION & PMF EVIDENCE GATES v1

Status: **DRAFT / VALIDATION AUTHORITY CANDIDATE**  
Updated: **2026-09-15**

## Purpose

Translate Product Health into business investment decisions.

Product metrics answer whether value appears.
This document answers:

> **WHEN IS THE EVIDENCE STRONG ENOUGH TO INVEST MORE?**

---

# 1. Evidence ladder

```text
ATTENTION
→ QUALIFIED EXPERIENCE
→ CONTINUATION
→ RETURN
→ EXPLICIT INTENT
→ COMMITMENT
→ PAYMENT
→ FULFILLMENT / USAGE
→ REPEAT PURCHASE
→ RECURRING NEED
→ SCALABLE ECONOMICS
```

Do not skip levels by interpretation.

---

# 2. Gate A — Proof of Attention

Question:

> Can we repeatedly bring relevant external people to a concrete Thing?

Required evidence:

- external/test population separated;
- source/intent reasonably known;
- real Thing destination;
- no forced account/membership prerequisite.

Not enough:

- impressions;
- raw pageviews;
- internal traffic;
- accidental virality.

Decision:

**PASS** → optimize experience, not scale media yet.

---

# 3. Gate B — Proof of Experience

Question:

> Do people actually consume/use the promised Thing?

Evidence depends on Form.

Examples:

- game starts / meaningful action;
- course experience begins;
- text consumed beyond accidental open;
- Event detail reaches a relevant action;
- Tool is used.

Not enough:

- click;
- open;
- scroll by itself.

Decision:

**PASS** → test continuation.

---

# 4. Gate C — Proof of Continuation

Question:

> After value, does another relevant Dementor object make sense to people?

Evidence:

- Thing→Thing;
- Project continuation;
- body-of-work continuation;
- meaningful share;
- next release interest.

Not enough:

- generic nav click;
- forced carousel;
- Home bounce-around.

Decision:

**PASS** → Product begins behaving like a Program, not isolated pages.

---

# 5. Gate D — Proof of Return

Question:

> Do independent people return in the expectation window because they remember value?

Evidence:

- direct / owned return;
- return to expected continuation;
- return payoff;
- repeated use of Practice/Tool.

No universal D7/D30 target before loop expectations are defined.

Decision:

**PASS** → recurring value hypothesis becomes eligible for study, not launch.

---

# 6. Gate E — Proof of Willingness to Pay

Question:

> Will a person exchange real money for a concrete value object?

Evidence hierarchy:

```text
interest click
< waitlist / request
< reservation / deposit
< payment
```

Only payment is payment evidence.

Partner/sponsor money proves partner-funded value, not automatically user willingness to pay.

Decision:

**PASS** → evaluate fulfillment economics.

---

# 7. Gate F — Proof of Paid Value

Question:

> Did paid users actually receive and use what was promised?

Required:

- fulfillment;
- usage / attendance;
- failure/refund record;
- qualitative outcome where relevant.

A payment without usage is not full product evidence.

Decision:

**PASS** → repeat-purchase test becomes valid.

---

# 8. Gate G — Proof of Repeat Purchase

Question:

> Will people voluntarily pay again for another concrete value object?

Preferred evidence:

- repeat purchase across independent people;
- no forced subscription;
- no extreme discount dependency;
- repeat after successful fulfillment.

Decision:

**PASS** → recurring package may be designed.

---

# 9. Gate H — Proof of Recurring Need

Question:

> Is there a recurring value package people genuinely need repeatedly?

Membership test:

> Remove the word “membership”. Is there still a clear recurring package worth paying for?

Evidence:

- repeat use;
- repeat payment;
- identifiable cadence;
- predictable fulfillment capacity;
- clear cancellation expectation.

Decision:

**PASS** → recurring pricing experiment allowed.

---

# 10. Gate I — Proof of Scalable Economics

Only after repeat exists evaluate:

- CAC;
- gross contribution;
- payback;
- churn / retention;
- LTV;
- capacity expansion cost;
- channel saturation.

Scale only if:

```text
VALUE QUALITY HOLDS
AND
FULFILLMENT QUALITY HOLDS
AND
UNIT ECONOMICS HOLD
```

No paid acquisition scaling merely because CAC looks low on initial conversion.

---

# 11. Decision statuses

Every experiment ends with one:

- **PASS — invest next stage**;
- **ITERATE — value exists, mechanism weak**;
- **HOLD — insufficient sample/evidence**;
- **FAIL — hypothesis not supported**;
- **INVALID — measurement/sample compromised**.

Avoid “promising”.

---

# 12. Stop conditions

Stop / redesign when:

- attention comes but experience does not;
- experience occurs but no meaningful continuation across multiple Things;
- return depends mainly on notification debt;
- paid intent disappears at real price;
- payment succeeds but usage/fulfillment is poor;
- repeat requires deep discounts;
- delivery capacity breaks quality;
- legal/payment constraints make the experiment materially different from the intended product.

---

# 13. PMF language discipline

Do not claim Product-Market Fit from:

- strong interviews;
- social praise;
- one viral Thing;
- one sold-out tiny Event;
- internal users;
- founder network only;
- one sponsor;
- one repeated buyer.

Working PMF evidence requires repeated patterns across independent external cohorts.

Until then use:

- `value signal`;
- `segment signal`;
- `paid signal`;
- `repeat signal`;
- `recurring signal`.

---

# 14. Review cadence

Weekly:

- experiment validity;
- qualitative surprises;
- data quality.

Monthly:

- evidence ladder by segment / Thing / offer;
- invest / iterate / stop decisions.

Quarterly:

- whether market / business model hypotheses should change.

---

# Final rule

> **INVESTMENT FOLLOWS EVIDENCE JUST AS MONETIZATION FOLLOWS VALUE.**
