# DC-9 Editorial QA Rubric

Status: **DRAFT WORKING STANDARD**

Every candidate question receives a compact editorial score before playtest.

## 1. Recognizability — 1–5

Can a person understand the situation immediately without knowing Dementor Club theory?

- 1 — abstract / theoretical;
- 3 — understandable but generic;
- 5 — concrete, familiar, easy to imagine oneself in.

## 2. Real conflict — 1–5

Does the question contain an actual tradeoff rather than four stylistic ways to say the same thing?

- 1 — no cost or conflict;
- 3 — mild tradeoff;
- 5 — two or more plausible values genuinely collide.

## 3. Non-obviousness — 1–5

Can the user easily spot the socially approved / “correct” answer?

- 1 — obvious moral ladder;
- 3 — one answer still looks notably wiser;
- 5 — all four options are psychologically plausible and no option announces itself as the test winner.

## 4. Dementor specificity — 1–5

Does score 3 show loss of power of successful-success, rather than ordinary competence?

- 1 — generic self-help / therapy / management advice;
- 3 — partially relevant;
- 5 — clear sphere-specific dismantling of an obligation to perform success.

## 5. Answer balance — 1–5

Are options similar enough in length, tone and sophistication that score cannot be inferred from writing quality?

- 1 — score 3 is obviously the longest/smartest;
- 3 — some imbalance;
- 5 — options feel like real competing behaviors.

## 6. Brevity — 1–5

Does the scene create enough context with minimal reading?

- 1 — exhausting;
- 3 — acceptable;
- 5 — one screen, immediate comprehension, no lost diagnostic context.

## 7. Emotional tension — 1–5

Is there enough stake to make the choice interesting?

- 1 — sterile;
- 3 — mildly engaging;
- 5 — user can feel the awkwardness, temptation, risk or cost without melodrama.

## 8. Replay resistance — 1–5

After several questions, can the user simply reverse-engineer “choose the most Dementor answer”?

- 1 — transparent ladder;
- 3 — partly gameable;
- 5 — meaning is contextual, options are mixed, presentation does not leak mechanics.

## Editorial total

Maximum: `40`.

Working interpretation:

- `34–40` — strong candidate for playtest;
- `29–33` — usable after focused edit;
- `23–28` — rewrite required;
- `<23` — reject or rebuild from a new scene.

A high total does NOT override a fatal semantic issue. A question is rejected even with a good total if:

- score 3 is only generic maturity/competence;
- the scene is harmful or irresponsible when taken literally;
- the public wording exposes score/tag/guard mechanics;
- answer position is statically tied to score;
- it duplicates another scene without adding evidence.

## Sphere-level QA

A sphere is ready for playtest when:

1. all four canonical tags have sufficient evidence;
2. no tag is represented only by abstract self-report;
3. at least one lower-stakes and one higher-stakes scene are present overall;
4. intentionality is behaviorally tested;
5. responsibility is behaviorally tested;
6. answer order can be shuffled without changing meaning;
7. presentation patterns vary enough to avoid repetitive reading;
8. total question count is justified by evidence coverage, not symmetry.
