# 01 Personality — Q1 Role Maintenance Candidate Lab

Status: **WORKBENCH / NOT APPROVED**

## Scene v0.1

Вы много лет были человеком, который в компании «обычно всё организует».

На этот раз друзья сами выбрали место, договорились о времени и всё забронировали.

В чат приходит сообщение:

> «Всё готово, увидимся завтра.»

Вас ни о чём не просят.

## What exactly are we measuring?

`role_maintenance / identity_service`

Question: when the environment no longer requires the familiar role, does the person allow the role to disappear, lightly keep contact with it, maintain it through a system, or defend it as a principle?

This is **not** a test of kindness, competence or whether the person is a good organizer.

## Impact

Candidate: `I3`.

Reason:

- low material cost;
- moderate identity/social stake;
- highly recognizable behavior;
- useful as an early low-risk scene before higher-stake guards.

Working coefficient: `.90`.

## Presentation

Primary: `P02 Message fragment`.

Optional supporting function: `P04 Witness` is already embedded in the message because the group explicitly confirms that everything is handled.

Diagnostic function: removes the practical excuse that intervention is necessary.

Satirical payoff is deferred; the question itself should remain nearly serious.

# Strategy clustering

## Cluster A — role released

Candidates: `R01`, `R02`.

Pattern logic:

The person accepts that the function is currently unnecessary and does not create a substitute reason to preserve it.

Candidate `pattern_stage: 0`.

Potential canonical meaning in Personality:

Old role has low power over the decision.

Candidate `canonical_evidence: 1.00`.

Important: this does **not** mean “0 is bad”. It shows why pattern stage and canonical evidence are separate axes.

---

## Cluster B — social/adaptive contact with role

Candidates: `R03`, `R04`, `R05`.

Pattern logic:

The person does not take over, but still checks whether their familiar function might be required or reassures themselves through a small verification.

Candidate `pattern_stage: 1`.

Potential canonical meaning:

The role still has some pull but does not control the situation.

Candidate `canonical_evidence: 0.67`.

---

## Cluster C — structured role preservation

Candidates: `R06`, `R07`, `R08`, `R09`.

Pattern logic:

The person creates a repeatable protection mechanism: silent checking, backup planning, risk scanning or unofficial control.

Candidate `pattern_stage: 2`.

Potential canonical meaning:

The role remains behaviorally active even without a request.

Candidate `canonical_evidence: 0.33`.

---

## Cluster D — role as principle

Candidates: `R10`, `R11`, `R12`.

Pattern logic:

The person can defend continued involvement as a general rule: if I can prevent failure / see the whole picture / know how, then stepping away would itself be irresponsible.

Candidate `pattern_stage: 3`.

Potential canonical meaning:

The identity/function retains strong authority over the decision.

Candidate `canonical_evidence: 0.00`.

This is the key v0.3 demonstration:

> `pattern_stage 3 ≠ Dementor score 3`.

# QA pass v0.1

## What separates 0 from 1?

`0` fully releases the role because it is unnecessary.

`1` does not take over, but performs a small social check that keeps the role available.

Boundary is not “good vs bad”; it is absence vs light preservation of the familiar function.

## What separates 2 from 3?

`2` uses a system: silent verification + backup plan.

`3` has a principle: competent people should intervene when they can prevent problems.

Boundary is mechanism vs worldview.

## Price of choice

Moderate identity/social discomfort, low material risk.

That is intentional: Q1 should establish the pattern cheaply before higher-stake questions test it under money/status/commitment.

## Can the Club-preferred answer be guessed?

Current risk: **medium**.

Candidate A may still look suspiciously “Dementor” because it is the cleanest refusal to organize.

Mitigation for next edit:

- make A slightly more ordinary and less ideologically pure;
- ensure D does not sound controlling or obviously unhealthy;
- consider making C professionally attractive enough that many competent users genuinely choose it.

## Do answers sound like real people?

A — yes.

B — yes.

C — yes, especially for organizers / project people.

D — plausible, but needs tonal polish so it sounds like a reasonable responsibility principle rather than a test villain.
